Kristine Pashin

I’m pursuing a joint doctorate at Oxford and the NIH, working on neural tissue engineering and machine learning for Parkinson’s disease. I’m interested in how we can build better models of human neural tissue and use them to understand disease and develop treatments.

Before this, I studied Symbolic Systems and Public Policy at Stanford, where I worked on hydrogels for growing and transplanting neural tissue. My work also spans bioethics and art history, from studying the regulation of stem cell clinics to researching how artists have represented pain. My art history thesis, The Shape of Suffering, explored these representations across cultures and periods, and I’m continuing this work through projects connecting art, medicine, and personal experiences of suffering.
